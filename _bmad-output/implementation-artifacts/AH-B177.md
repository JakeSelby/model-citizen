## Reproduction

The first paid long-session pilot (exploratory: one scenario, bare and harness arms on
`claude-sonnet-5`, one rep) counted 4.67 USD against 1.11 USD spent, so the session cap stopped
both sessions after 8 and 9 of 26 user turns. On all 17 turns, the change in a result's
`total_cost_usd` from the previous turn equals that turn's own top-level `usage` priced at the
model's rates, to four decimals.

## Root cause

A resumed `claude -p --resume` turn reports `total_cost_usd` and `modelUsage` as the session's
running totals; only top-level `usage` is the turn's own. `replay_session.turn_usage` read the last
result's `total_cost_usd` and per-model `modelUsage` as the turn's own and `run_session` summed
them, inflating the session cost, the cap stop, the per-turn curves, the segment figures and
`cost_by_tier`. [Source: scripts/replay_session.py#turn_usage, docs/benchmarks.md#Long-session tier]

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Given** results carrying running totals, **when** a session runs, **then** each turn's cost is
   the change in `total_cost_usd` since the previous result, turn 1 against zero.
2. **Given** the same session, **then** its `cost_usd` is the last total and its cumulative curve
   is the totals.
3. **Given** per-model `modelUsage` totals, **then** a turn's tokens and `cost_by_tier` are the
   per-model, key-by-key changes, a model first seen counting against zero.
4. **Given** a cap, **then** the session stops before a turn only once the latest total reaches it.
5. **Given** a checkpoint, **then** its row's segment cost, tokens and tiers are the change across
   its segment.
6. **Given** any checkpoint check, **then** `/session-baseline.json` is mounted read-only holding
   `{"total_cost_usd", "modelUsage"}` from the last result before the segment, zero and empty for
   the first segment.
7. **Given** a turn that times out, **then** it still counts at the rest of the cap and errors the
   session.
8. **Given** a turn whose result names another session, or whose `total_cost_usd` or any model's
   `modelUsage` key falls below the previous turn's, **then** the session errors as `resume-lost`
   and the turn counts at the rest of the cap, never as a negative cost.

## Design

`replay_session.session_totals` reads a result's totals; `turn_usage` takes the previous totals and
returns the deltas plus its own totals for the next turn. `run_session` keeps the latest totals as
the spend and passes each segment's opening totals to `checker` as a third argument.
`cost_bench.session_baseline` writes them beside the segment stream, and `score`, `run_check` and
`replay_arms.check_command` mount the file read-only at `SESSION_BASELINE`. Segment figures stay
the sum of per-turn deltas, which telescopes to the change across the segment. Rejected: pricing
top-level `usage` locally, which needs a price table the CLI already applies. Must not change: the
baseline file's name or shape, which the evaluator pack reads, or the timeout rule.

## Regression test

`tests/test_replay_long_session_cost.py` drives a made-up five-turn session of running totals; on
the old code its per-turn costs were the totals themselves and its session cost their sum.

## Dev notes

- A resume that silently starts afresh shows as another session id or a falling total
  (`replay_session.totals_fell`); the turn's tokens and tiers are then unknown, like a timeout's.
  Only `RUNNING_TOTAL_KEYS` are compared: `contextWindow`, `maxOutputTokens`, `canonicalModel`,
  `costBasis` and `provider` describe the model and may change or vanish.
- `--max-budget-usd` counts the invocation's own spend, so the cap less the latest total is the
  right budget; the pilot ran a 0.33 USD turn to success from a 0.44 USD session total.
- A result with no `modelUsage` takes its tokens from its own top-level `usage`.
- The evaluator pack's segment metrics change in parallel to read the baseline file.
[Source: docs/benchmarks.md#Long-session tier]
